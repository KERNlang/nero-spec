## Historical context and limits

The earlier 12-case exercise graded **bug-detection points out of 36**, not a count of bugs. Its uncriticized and subagent-critic results were Nero Spec 23/27, Spec Kit 19/23.5, Kiro 23.5/28, and OpenSpec 19.5/24.5 points. The pilot's `cli-01` case scored 3/3 for both Nero and Spec Kit in that exercise; it was not selected as a known Nero win. That exercise graded specifications rather than developer implementations, forced a Spec Kit artifact package under its old protocol, and lacked false-alarm cases. [The score output](historical/bench6/score6_out.txt) contains its full matrix.

The later raw-writer-versus-Nero control used the same subagent critic in both arms and did not clearly establish an added structural benefit. Its reported arm-seed totals were raw 24.5 and 28, Nero 25 and 30 points out of a nominal 36; one case/seed pair lacked a grade and was excluded from both arms. Both arms varied substantially by seed, and the preregistered noise rule fired. See [its preregistration](historical/bench6/control/PREREG.md) and [score output](historical/bench6/control/scoreC_out.txt). None of those historical scores is a developer-pilot score.

This is one historical task, one seed, and the same model for both arms. Native process requirements differ, notably Nero's required prebuild critic. Local tests and independent review can each miss issues. The pilot supports a narrow observation: Nero's full native workflow handled one valid non-getter case in its first pass; Spec Kit handled it after one feedback repair. It does not isolate the value of Nero's template from its external critic calls, rank frameworks overall, or establish that context size caused the result. The earlier broad negative judgment of Nero was unsupported by the measured evidence. A lightweight direction remains defensible, but no shipping decision follows from this pilot alone. This report assigns no subjective aggregate score.
